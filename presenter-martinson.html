URiCA Fall Symposium 2026 Presenters

Negotiating Tradition and Innovation: Shamisen Practice in Contemporary Tokyo

Poster Location 4

Victor Martinson, Music major

Mentor: Robert Hodson, Music

Abstract: During seven weeks of fieldwork in Tokyo in summer 2026, I explored how the shamisen, a traditional Japanese three-stringed instrument, is taught, performed, and understood in contemporary Japan. My research focused on Nagauta, a style closely associated with Kabuki theater, while also examining Tsugaru shamisen and other musical traditions. A highlight of the project was studying with Imafuji Chojuro IV, the fourth-generation head (Iemoto) of the Imafuji school of Nagauta. Through private lessons, I experienced a teaching tradition that emphasizes imitation, repetition, and physical precision. I also observed professional performances, including Kabuki and Nagauta concerts, and accompanied the Iemoto to an NHK television recording session. As the project developed, I became interested in the different social settings in which shamisen music is performed. In addition to attending Tsugaru performances, I met Okinawan sanshin players and participated in informal playing sessions. These experiences broadened my original research questions and helped me understand how musical traditions can serve very different purposes, from formal theatrical performance to communal music-making. My poster presents observations from the fieldwork, focusing on differences in performance practice, teaching methods, and cultural context. I will also demonstrate Nagauta shamisen techniques and share audio and video examples of several musical styles. By combining research with live performance, I hope to show how these traditions continue to thrive and evolve in contemporary Japan.
