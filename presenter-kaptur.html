The State Sourced Trap: How Illinois DCFS Policies Lead to Sex Trafficking Exploitation

Poster Location 26

Brianna Kaptur, Social Work and Criminology major

Mentors: Terri Jones, Educational Opportunity Program; John Dominguez, Educational Opportunity Program; and Kiara Rios, UW-Whitewater Class of 2026

Abstract: The purpose of this paper is to examine the failure of the Illinois Department of Children and Family Services (DCFS) in adhering to Rule 329, which mandates that any missing foster children must be reported within 24 hours. The purpose of this paper is to discuss how the failure in administrative action makes the foster child susceptible to being trafficked for sex. Using federal audit data, neurobiological studies, and current legislative policies, the research analyzes the consequences of state neglect. Results indicated that the state failed to meet mandated reporting timelines in 45% of cases, directly correlating with increased instances of "survival labor" among displaced youth. Research findings indicated that the consequences of placement instability and delayed reports included toxic-stress-induced impairment of the prefrontal cortex and poor self-regulation among the victims. This research suggests that administrative silence plays a key role in facilitating predation, with an adverse effect on Black and Latinx youths through neglect based on their race. Recommendations include developing automated report systems and recognizing runaway youth as endangered victims.
