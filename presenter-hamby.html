URiCA Fall Symposium 2026 Presenters

Studying rock movements with sensors in real time during slope collapse

Poster Location 16

Chase Hamby, Environmental Science major; Andrew Veroeven, Environmental Science major; and Taylor Manne, Mathematics major

Mentor: Prajukti Bhattacharyya, Geography, Geology, and Environmental Science

Abstract: Landslides are dangerous events that occur due to plane failure in a surface causing massive amounts of land to be moved; this can be highly dangerous and highly unpredictable sometimes with little warning before they occur. An automated warning system being put into place could help warn those around slopes if there are signs of an impending landslide, finding a specific pre-collapse movement that could be found in landslide events could be the key to creating such a warning system. Through the use of a Tilebox Pro sensor, we can detect directional movement as it occurs during the experiment with our sensor taking 100 data points each second. In preliminary tests we have used a stream table to forcibly trigger a movement event, with plans to simulate landslide conditions through prolonged experiments using slopes constructed with sand, that are slowly inundated with water, to hopefully detect pre-collapse movement. Preliminary results show low level vibration can be seen prior to total collapse this is seen ~2 seconds before collapse; more data is needed to confirm whether this was noise due to testing conditions or if this data is significant. Upcoming work will include creating a more natural capsule for the sensor to better simulate natural conditions. Studying the events leading up to a landslide may give us important information on what we can look for to further develop early warning systems, allowing for better response when a landslide may be imminent.
