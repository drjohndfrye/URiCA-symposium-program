Design, Synthesis and Preclinical Evaluation of Boron-Based Niclosamide Prodrugs for Nonalcoholic Steatohepatitis

Poster Location 31

Megan Honn, Chemistry major and Emily Meyer, Biochemistry major

Mentors: Hephzibah Kumpaty, Chemistry and Shirisha Jonnalagadda, Chemistry

Abstract: Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is the leading cause of chronic liver disease, affecting approximately 20-30% of the U.S. population. Untreated NAFLD can progress to nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), which may lead to fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma. Despite its growing prevalence, effective pharmacological treatments for NASH remain limited. Mitochondrial uncouplers enhance cellular metabolism by dissipating the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, thereby promoting fatty acid oxidation and reducing intracellular lipid accumulation. Niclosamide, an FDA-approved anthelmintic drug, acts as a mitochondrial uncoupler and has been shown to activate AMPK signaling, enhance lipid metabolism, and attenuate NASH in preclinical models. However, its poor aqueous solubility, low oral bioavailability, and limited metabolic stability hinder its therapeutic development for NASH. To overcome these limitations, we hypothesize that boronic acid-based prodrugs of niclosamide will exhibit improved pharmaceutical and pharmacokinetic properties suitable for clinical translation. In this regard, a series of niclosamide derivatives were synthesized and evaluated for biological activity. The lead candidates exhibited limited antiproliferative activity in MDA-MB-231 and HepG2 cells, suggesting reduced cytotoxicity and improved suitability for chronic administration. Importantly, mechanistic studies in HepG2 cells revealed that these compounds increased AMPK expression, consistent with the proposed mitochondrial uncoupling mechanism of niclosamide and the therapeutic modulation of lipid metabolism. These findings identify promising boron-based niclosamide prodrugs for further development as NASH therapeutics and will be presented at the conference.
