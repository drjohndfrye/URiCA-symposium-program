The Homosexual Threat Arrives On Campus: Robert Russell Judson, Medicalized Surveillance, and the Midwestern Lavender Scare

Poster Location 20

Alison Bishop, History major

Mentors: Ashley Barnes-Gilbert, Women and Gender Studies and Karl Brown, Hisotry

Abstract: The 1949 disciplinary case of University of Missouri student Robert Russell Judson demonstrates how mid-century higher education used medical understandings of homosexuality to construct a surveillance system on campus. Targeted for allegedly attending a “gay” gathering in Kansas City, Judson was investigated by a specialized university team combining academic disciplinarians, police science instructors, and student health psychiatrists. Defining homosexuality as an incurable pathology and social contagion, administrators subjected Judson to psychological profiling, coercive interrogations, and threats of a “truth serum.” When Judson refused to confess or name fellow students, university officials cancelled his registration and placed a restrictive hold on his permanent transcript: "Do not allow to enter without consent of the Committee on Discipline." Despite appeals to President Middlebush and the Board of Curators, Judson was effectively blacklisted. This mark blocked credit transfers, terminated his advanced R.O.T.C. officer training, and ruined his career prospects. Crucially, Judson’s experience revals that Cold War anti-gay purges were neither isolated occurrences nor restricted to coastal political hubs, where this history is traditionally centered. Rather, Judon was victimized by a standardized, interstate institutional program. The University of Missouri modeled its anti-gay protocals on established practices done at the University of Wisconsin-Madison. By shifting analytical focus to the Midwest, Judson’s case demonstrates how heartland public universities operated as critical laboratories for medicalized surveillance, formulating the administrative and legal blueprints that linked educational exclusion, career-blocking, and federal security policy during the Lavender Scare.
