URiCA Fall Symposium 2026 Presenters

Detailed Analysis of a Central Wisconsin Pegmatite and Implications for Critical Element Exploration

Poster Location 17

Kybie Roys, Environmental Science major and Ghost Stoner, Geography major

Mentor: Prajukti Bhattacharyya, Geography, Geology, and Environmental Science

Abstract: Critical elements, such as Rare Earth Elements (REEs) and High Field Strength Elements (HFSEs), have high societal and economic importance for technological advancement. The conditions concentrating these elements into economic deposits need detailed study. Pegmatites are common structures enriched with REE and HFSE bearing fluids leading to unusual mineral formations. This research focuses on a pegmatite from the Stettin Complex in Central Wisconsin to understand how REEs may have been concentrated in different minerals. The Stettin Complex is the oldest (1565 Ma) and most alkaline of the four intrusions of the Wausau Syenite Complex (WSC). Previous work on the WSC and associated pegmatites have shown presence of REE-bearing minerals. Our studied pegmatite may provide important information about REE mobilization. Five samples labeled A-E were collected from different parts of the pegmatite. Minerals in hand sample scale show significant size variation, from submillimeter to centimeter scale. Sample A displays zones of coloration and textural change that may imply fluid flow. In order to conduct detailed chemical and mineralogical analysis, a rotary tool was used for precise extraction of materials to be analyzed by Powder X-Ray Diffraction (PXRD). Preliminary mineralogical data from PXRD aligns with previous work. These data will be complemented by future chemical analysis to identify any economically viable critical elements. In this poster we present our findings and implications for future research.
