The relationship between rumination and social media use as predictors of forgiveness in college students.

Poster Location 32

Alanna Logemann, Psychology major and Marah Vogelsberg, Psychology major

Mentor: Jiahe Wang Xu, Psychology

Abstract: Forgiveness, social media, and rumination all play a significant role in mental health, although there are limited studies that examine the relationship between these three variables. This study aims to look at how forgiveness, as a beneficial process and goal, is impacted by social media and rumination. We hypothesized that rumination and social media use (SMU) were negatively correlated with the three subscales of forgiveness (affect, behavior, and cognition). We also hypothesized that social media use was positively correlated with rumination. Results show that social media use was positively correlated with rumination, and negatively correlated with affective forgiveness and cognitive forgiveness. Rumination was negatively correlated with all three of the forgiveness subscales. Our findings support the majority of our hypotheses. We also ran a regression analysis examining whether rumination and social media use would uniquely explain variance in forgiveness. Additionally, we conducted exploratory independent-samples t-tests to examine whether men and women differ in their scores on rumination, social media use, and the three dimensions of forgiveness (affect, behavior, and cognition). Finally, as an exploratory analysis, we divided participants into high- and low-social-media-use groups based on a median split and compared the groups on forgiveness and rumination.
