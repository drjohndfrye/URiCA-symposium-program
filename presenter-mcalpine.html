Was the Yellowstone Wolf Reintroduction Successful in the Eyes of Researchers and Local Outfitters?

Poster Location 28

Selah McAlpine, Biology major

Mentor: John Frye, Geography, Geology, and Environmental Science

Abstract: The Yellowstone National Park wolf reintroduction of 1995 is one of the most historical moments in National Park history, but there have been opposing views about whether the wolves have become a problem. These opposing views came from researchers and National Park specialists who believe the wolf reintroduction went perfectly, and local ranchers who believe that the wolves are too abundant. The research consisted of many peer reviewed articles, population trend analysis, and testimony from local ranchers and Yellowstone specialists to help determine if the wolf population is growing to be an issue, or if there have been blind spots in research post-reintroduction. The results showed that the populations of elk, wolves, and deciduous plant species have all shown stability in recent years, and the most common reason for local ranchers to dislike wolves is because of their declining hunting success. Although there are opposing sides, the ultimate consensus is that there has been a huge blind spot in wolf population research surrounding Yellowstone National Park, so it is likely that future research will start to stretch to the entire Greater Yellowstone Ecosystem rather than just inside the park.
