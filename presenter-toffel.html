URiCA Fall Symposium 2026 Presenters

Seeking Joy in Teaching and Learning

Poster Location 6

Elizabeth Toffel, Art and Elementary/Middle Education major

Mentor: Veronica Soria-Martinez, Curriculum and Instruction

Abstract: In this study, we examine ways in which care, students’ sense of belonging, and joy in learning are interrelated processes. For the purpose of this study, care is considered in three ways: interpersonal care between teachers and students; collective care among students; and disciplinary care of students for the class content. To examine the relationships among these processes, we look at categories related to care, such as promoting students’ voices and establishing trusting relationships, and how they interact with categories related to developing a sense of belonging, which involves students feeling validated and affirmed. Then we analyze how those categories interact with indicators associated with joy in learning, such as intrinsic motivation, participation beyond established requirements, and expression of feelings and emotions. Other contextual categories include student agency and opening space gradually for students to make curricular choices in the space of a community-based learning (CBL) approach. We use data from the preservice art educator classroom, including classroom participation and students’ contributions, to look at students’ interactions and dialogue. Similarly, we look at student-made artifacts to look into how students engage intellectually. Data reveals how students express care for each other through peer feedback, collaboration, disagreement, encouragement, or probing each other’s assumptions. Our findings show that dialogical practices provide opportunities for students to show a shared investment in learning, developing a sense of care, belonging, and joy.
