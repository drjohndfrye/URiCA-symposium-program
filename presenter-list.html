Exploring Long-Term Outcomes in an Inclusive Post-Secondary Transition Program

Emma Aleckson, Communication Sciences and Disorders major

Mentor: Sara Athorp, Special Education/LIFE Program

Poster Location: 9

Abstract: In this research, I am exploring the long-term outcomes of students who have completed an inclusive postsecondary education program at a mid-sized public university in the Midwest. Specifically, I am examining independent living, employment, and everyday gains that students achieve after graduation. Students in inclusive postsecondary education programs have intellectual disabilities that affect intellectual functioning, academic achievement, and adaptive behavior. Because these disabilities influence many aspects of their lives, it is important to examine independence across multiple domains. Previous researchers have examined these outcome markers for similar programs across the United States. Many studies have focused on employment outcomes and found a higher percentage of employed individuals with intellectual disabilities compared to the national average after completing such programs. As a student employee of the program at my university, I wish to identify and describe the post-graduation outcomes of our alumni. To accomplish this, I will contact all alumni who have completed either the two-year or four-year program and invite them to complete a survey. Once the survey results are collected, I will work with my mentor to develop a coding system to categorize similar responses and analyze patterns across the data. The findings will strengthen the evidence supporting the benefits of inclusive postsecondary education programs and provide valuable insights into our program’s current strengths and areas for improvement.
