Cybersecurity Awareness Levels among Minority-Owned Small Businesses: Obstacles and Opportunities

Poster Location 14

Omarian Hughes, Cybersecurity major

Mentor: Roger Yin, Information Technology and Business Education

Abstract: Small businesses are among the most frequent targets of cyberattacks, yet minority-owned small businesses face an even steeper challenge. They deal with the same growing threats while carrying heavier financial and structural burdens than their counterparts. The central problems this research addresses are that minority small business owners may not be fully aware of the ongoing cyber risks targeting them, and even when they are, real barriers stand in the way of delaying responses. This study asks two questions: Are minority small business owners aware of the most common cyberattacks? And what is preventing them from protecting their businesses? This research matters because minority-owned businesses are a vital part of the American economy, and a single cyberattack can shut down an organization permanently. Using a survey-based design methodology, data were collected directly from minority small business owners to measure their awareness levels and identify the obstacles they face. Early findings suggest that limited budgets, lack of cybersecurity training, unequal access to capital, and low awareness of free government resources are the biggest barriers. These results point to a clear need for targeted outreach, affordable training programs, and policy changes that account for the unique challenges faced by minority business owners. Future research should increase the sample size and explore community-based solutions to close this gap.
