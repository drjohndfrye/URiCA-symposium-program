The role of visibility and gait in affordance duration perception

Poster Location 24

Bryce Faith, Psychology major and Alanna Logemann, Psychology major

Mentor: Brandon Thomas, Psychology

Abstract: Previous research examined the role of visibility and gait in affordance distance perception, but there remains a gap in studying those roles in affordance duration perception. Affordance duration perception is one’s ability to perceive the time it takes to complete an action. Our study aims to examine the role of visibility and gait on affordance duration perception. To do so, we measured out and created a route with cones and strings that starts in front of one of the partitions that is able to be raised and lowered located in the volleyball gym at UW-Whitewater’s Kachel Fieldhouse. We asked participants to take a view of the route and then time themselves perceiving how long it will take to walk the route at a specific speed and then measured the participants' affordance duration. We repeated this process three times manipulating both visibility, speed, and their gait switching between a walk and gallop. Previous research shows when changing to an unfamiliar gait and limited visibility, participants find it more difficult to accurately perceive distance, but when compared to a familiar gait, such as walking, participants are accurate at distance perception. This suggests that there’s a deeper understanding of our ability to perceive the environment around us. With manipulating participants' gait to something unfamiliar and blocking visibility participants will become inaccurate at affordance duration perception, suggesting there’s an underlying variable to our ability to perceive the duration it takes to perform actions within our environment that isn’t a “ticking clock” inside our consciousness.
