Does the Child Tax Credit Promote Family Stability and Reduce Student Homelessness? Evidence on Children's Living Arrangement

Poster Location 27

Emmalee Empey, Mathematics and Economics major

Mentor: Sakshi Bhardwaj, Economics

Abstract: This project examines the impact of Child Tax Credit (CTC) expansions on children's living arrangements. We study two major reforms: the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act and the temporary 2021 expansion under the American Rescue Plan Act. Using the American Community Survey and the Survey of Income and Program Participation, we exploit variation in CTC exposure based on the number of eligible children and estimate difference-in-differences and event-study models. Preliminary evidence from the 2017 expansion shows that greater CTC exposure increased children's likelihood of living with both parents and reduced single-parent residence, living with neither parent, and being in grandparent or other-relative care, suggesting that income support may promote family stability. Analysis of the 2021 CTC expansion is ongoing.
