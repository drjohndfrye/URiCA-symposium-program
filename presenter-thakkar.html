Understanding Financial Market Risk During Economic Uncertainty: Preliminary Evidence from U.S. Financial and Economic Data

Poster Location 7

Jainil Thakkar, Business Analytics major

Mentor: Charu Rajapaksha, Mathematics

Abstract: Understanding how financial markets respond to changing economic conditions is essential for evaluating market risk and uncertainty. This ongoing undergraduate research project examines the relationships between U.S. stock-market volatility and major economic indicators using publicly available data from October 2016 through October 2026. Data from the Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED) database and Cboe Global Markets were integrated into a monthly dataset containing S&P 500 returns, realized volatility, the Volatility Index (VIX), inflation, interest rates, unemployment, and industrial production. Preliminary analyses were conducted in R using descriptive statistics, time-series visualizations, and Pearson correlation analysis, with careful consideration of missing observations. Initial findings indicate a strong positive correlation between VIX and S&P 500 realized volatility ((r=0.89)), while monthly stock-market returns ((r=-0.37)) and industrial production growth ((r=-0.29)) exhibit negative associations with realized volatility. In contrast, inflation shows a weak positive correlation ((r=0.10)). Time-series visualizations reveal substantial increases in market volatility during the 2020 economic disruption, highlighting the importance of extreme events in understanding financial risk. These preliminary results suggest that financial-market risk is associated with both market-based uncertainty measures and broader economic conditions, although the strength of these relationships varies across indicators. This presentation summarizes the data preparation, exploratory analysis, and initial findings rather than the completed study. Future research will incorporate regression modeling, time-series diagnostics, and sensitivity analyses to further investigate these relationships and assess their stability across different economic conditions
