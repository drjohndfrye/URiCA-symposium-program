Silica and Alkalinity Variations with Age in the Wausau Syenite Complex

Poster Location 19

Ghost Stoner, Geography major; Andrew Veroeven, Environmental Science major; and Kybie Roys, Environmental Science major

Mentor: Prajukti Bhattacharyya, Geography, Geology, and Environmental Science

Abstract: The Wausau Syenite Complex (WSC) in north-central Wisconsin is a series of igneous intrusions. It consists of four plutons ranging in age from 1565-1506 Ma: from oldest to youngest, these are the Stettin complex, and the Wausau, Rib Mountain, and Nine Mile plutons. Each pluton is mineralogically and chemically heterogenous; the Stettin complex is most alkaline, and the Nine Mile pluton is most silicic, suggesting variations with age. Though this relationship is clearly observable, its cause is unknown. The goal of this project is to expand upon prior research on the mineralogical and chemical heterogeneity of the WSC's plutons and to explore the implications this has for the formation of these intrusive bodies. Powdered X-Ray Diffraction (PXRD) and petrographic thin selection analysis were performed on samples from the WSC. Quartz, albite, and orthoclase are commonly seen in all analyzed samples. Results from thin section analysis showed variable cooling rates in all analyzed samples reflected by variations in grain size. Wausau pluton samples showed consistently small crystals, while samples from the Nine Mile pluton possessed generally larger grains. Future work will involve detailed chemical analysis with a handheld X-Ray Fluorescence (XRF) analyzer and Inductively Coupled Plasma Optical Emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES) to identify chemical characteristics of each sample. This will complement previous data from PXRD and thin section analysis, and provide further information about the chemical heterogeneity of the intrusions. In this poster, we present our findings and implications for future research.
