Photocatalysis Degradation of Organic Pollutants from Water with a Nanomaterial Heterojunction

Poster Location 21

Heriberto Patino Luna, Physics and Geography major and Nathan Kats, Chemistry major

Mentor: Paul House, Chemistry

Abstract: Due to the electronic structure of semiconductors, light of a wavelength corresponding to the semiconductors' bandgap can be absorbed and excite an electron to the conduction band, leaving a hole behind. These charge carriers can degrade organic pollutants themselves and react with water to produce reactive oxygen species (ROS), which oxidatively degrade organic pollutants. This process is known as photocatalytic degradation. Our main objective is to investigate the degradation mechanisms of metal oxide photocatalysts from repeated cycle use. Gaining a full understanding of the degradation mechanisms will be beneficial for practical, large-scale applications of these materials, including in water treatment plants for the degradation of organic pollutants. The organic pollutant that we are trying to degrade is perfluorooctanoic acid (PFOA), a type of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS). Initial experiments attempt to create a heterojunction formed with titanium dioxide (TiO2) and molybdenum trioxide (MoO3). TiO2 was commercially bought, while MoO3 was synthesized through Joule heating synthesis. The materials individually have been characterized with scanning electron microcopy (SEM), X-ray diffraction (XRD), micro-Raman spectroscopy, and UV-Vis spectroscopy. We are currently trying to find the pH at which the surface potential of the two materials is of opposite polarity through zeta potential measurements. This is important to form the heterojunction through electrostatic self-assembly. Once the heterojunction has been formed, the next steps include conducting cyclic photocatalyst experiments. We hope to create three heterojunctions and run through 50 cycles of photocatalysis or until the rate constant drops below 50% of its original value. The progress of photodegradation will be found by taking aliquots every 20 minutes during the 2-hour photocatalysis experiments. Post-use characterization with UV-Vis, TEM, and XPS will give insights into the degradation mechanism of the photocatalyst heterojunction.
