Rules, Norms, Values and the corresponding Hierarchy within the rehearsal space in the University Band at UWW

Poster Location 13

Victoria Pudlo, Criminology major

Mentor: Tarryl Janik, Anthropology

Abstract: This research examines the rules, norms, values, and hierarchy of the University Band at the University of Wisconsin–Whitewater (UWW) during the spring semester of 2026, from January 26 to April 20. The concert cycle included performances on March 9 and April 20 in Young Auditorium. Having played the flute for ten years, I chose to study the ensemble to examine familiar musical practices from an outsider’s perspective and reconsider what I had come to view as “normal.” I used participant observation as my primary research method, participating in rehearsals while recording physical and mental notes about the ensemble’s practices and interactions. My observations revealed a consistent rehearsal schedule and established rules governing rehearsal conduct. The ensemble’s norms centered on three primary pillars: inclusivity, preparation, and respect. A defined hierarchy also shaped how the band operated both during rehearsals and on stage. Additionally, the ensemble’s values fell into two categories: personal values, which the director, referred to as Doc, instilled in individual members, and group values, which emphasized the ensemble’s collective identity and public representation. These findings suggest that the band’s interconnected rules, norms, values, and hierarchy foster a shared sense of purpose and create an immersive musical experience through the dedication and collaboration of its members. Future research could examine interactions and behaviors in concert settings more closely and compare the University Band with other musical ensembles at UWW.
