Macroeconomic and Infrastructural Determinants of National Tennis Success: A Panel Analysis of ATP Rankings

Poster Location 8

Gavin Eckerman, Economics, Business Analytics, Public Policy and Administration, and Mathematics major

Mentor: Russel Kashian, Economics

Abstract: This paper evaluates the macro-level determinants of national success in men's professional tennis using global ATP ranking data from 1974 to 2024. Employing panel regressions across top-100 and top-1000 point systems, we find that urban population percentage and the share of the population aged 50 and over are the primary determinants of international success, while baseline nominal GDP is statistically insignificant in modern periods. Cross-sectional estimations and Hausman tests reveal significant structural breaks between the early period (1974–1989) and later periods (1994–2024). These shifts demonstrate that the rapid globalization of tennis and localized infrastructure access have effectively replaced national wealth as the main driver of elite athletic output, suggesting that targeted development pipelines matter more than aggregate economic resources.
