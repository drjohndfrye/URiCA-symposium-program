Evaluating Real-Time English-to-Spanish Machine Translation in Live Subtitling

Poster Location 10

Lily Everts, Communication Sciences and Disorders major and Kenzie Nielsen, Communication Sciences and Disorders major

Mentor: Lynn Gilbertson, Communication Sciences and Disorders

Abstract: Speech-language pathologists, audiologists, and educators in the instructional setting have increasing access to AI to support Universal Design for Learning (UDL) and multimodal access for diverse learners. However, the instructional reliability of real-time, cross-linguistic translation remains under-researched. This descriptive pilot project evaluates the accuracy of Microsoft PowerPoint’s real-time, English-to-Spanish and Spanish-to-English auto-generated subtitles to explore their potential for bilingual service delivery and classroom implementation. Prior research indicates that automated captioning accuracy fluctuates based on a speaker’s vocal frequency, amplitude, and sociolinguistic characteristics. To establish a baseline, PowerPoint’s subtitle feature will be evaluated across four adult speaker profiles (native English-speaking male/female and native Spanish-speaking male/female) reading a phonetically balanced text. Accuracy will be quantified by comparing AI-generated subtitles against the original recordings. Ultimately, these findings will provide professionals in educational settings with data-driven insights into the accuracy of real-time AI subtitles and their potential to support communication access.
