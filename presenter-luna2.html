URiCA Fall Symposium 2026 Presenters

A Computational Study of the Olfactory Bulb in the Mammal Brain

Poster Location 22

Heriberto Patiño Luna, Physics and Geography major

Mentor: Martin Tchernookov, Physics

Abstract: The olfactory bulb is the first site in the brain where information processing and refinement about a smell occurs. It receives information from olfactory sensory neurons (OSN) located inside the nasal cavity. The axons of OSNs transmit signal to the olfactory bulb cells’ dendrites, such as mitral cells (MCs). There are granule cells (GSs) attached to these MCs that receive information from the MCs and inhibit the MCs, resulting in the reshaping of the original signal. It is believed that the survival of GCs is largely due to the frequency of their stimulation by the MCs. In this study, we aim to investigate whether a mutual information-based framework can accurately describe the mechanisms occurring in the olfactory bulb system. More precisely, we want to test the hypothesis that the MCs do not resolve all the signal from the OSNs’ stimuli alone; they need the GCs to do so. Mutual information (MI) maximization models have been used in the past to explain neural interactions in early olfactory structures. In this work, we aim to compare the mutual information performance of a MC-GS model against the channel capacity of an equivalent linear system. The main computational goal is calculating the MI of a Gaussian signal and the MC response using output linearization and analytical results about the MI established earlier. Recent efforts have been on the linearization procedure. This has been done by calculating the autocorrelation, a measure of how much signal changes over time, and from it extracting a data pruning parameter.
