Functional Analysis of Brugia malayi DAF-2

Poster Location 29

Megan Miller, Biology major

Mentor: Kirsten Crossgrove, Biology

Abstract: Brugia malayi are parasitic worms that can infect humans and cause lymphatic filariasis. We are interested in studying the role of the insulin/insulin-like growth factor receptor signaling (IIS) pathway in B. malayi development. This study aims to confirm that proteins found in the respective IIS pathways of B. malayi and the model organism Caenorhabditis elegans, Bma-DAF-2 and Ce-DAF-2, serve similar functional purposes. In C. elegans, DAF-2 functions as a receptor for insulin-like ligands. We performed HiFi assembly to combine fragments of the Bma-daf-2 gene with a eukaryotic expression vector. However, we isolated no assembled clones, so we are currently individually cloning and sequencing the three fragments to evaluate why the assembly did not work. We will then express Bma-DAF-2 in cell culture to test its function. This will help us to have a deeper understanding of whether the insulin/insulin-like growth factor receptor signaling (IIS) pathway helps regulate development of the infective stage (iL3) in B. malayi, similar to its role in regulating the dauer stage in C. elegans.
