Geochemical Experiments on Maghemite Formation to Infer Martian Surface Conditions

Poster Location 18

Andrew Veroeven, Environmental Science major

Mentor: Prajukti Bhattacharyya, Geography, Geology, and Environmental Science

Abstract: Maghemite is a weathering product that forms as magnetite transitions to other iron oxide minerals. Although maghemite has been observed on Earth as well as on Mars through rover exploration and remote sensing, how maghemite forms naturally is not yet clearly understood. Since it has been observed on Mars, the conditions of maghemite formation can be used to make inferences about Martian surface processes. In this research, we use controlled experiments to study maghemite formation from magnetite to better understand how it may occur on Mars. Preliminary results from the reaction between magnetite and hydrogen peroxide showed the strongest evidence of maghemite formation. Continued experimentation includes various solutions to react with magnetite: A.) 35% hydrogen peroxide, B.) 29.4ppt sodium chloride solution to simulate seawater, C.) sodium hydroxide solution with a pH of ~8.5, and D.) a 1:1 mix of solutions B and C. One vial each of solutions A, B, C, and D was placed in a water bath at 50C for 6 weeks. The temperature was then increased to 75C for 3 additional weeks. Solution A in the hot water bath showed the production of maghemite after 9 weeks. Future work will include repeating experiments with solution A on a larger scale and extending the duration of the experiment. Additionally, we will conduct similar experiments on iron-rich basalts from the Reykjanes Peninsula in Iceland as a proxy for Martian bedrock material.
